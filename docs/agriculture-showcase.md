# AI Showcase: Transforming Agriculture

Artificial Intelligence is not limited to software and digital realms; it is making significant physical impacts in traditional industries like agriculture. The integration of AI, IoT, and robotics is creating the era of **Precision Agriculture**.

## Computer Vision for Crop Health

One of the most impactful applications of AI in agriculture is the use of computer vision to monitor crop health. Drones and automated rovers equipped with high-resolution cameras capture images of fields. AI models trained on vast datasets of plant diseases can analyze these images in real-time.

![AI Monitoring Fruits and Vegetables](./assets/buah-sayur.jpg)
*AI systems can detect ripeness, disease, and yield estimates from visual data.*

This allows farmers to identify diseases, nutrient deficiencies, or pest infestations days or weeks before they become visible to the naked eye. Early detection means targeted treatment, reducing crop loss and saving resources.

## Precision Spraying and Automation

Traditional pesticide and fertilizer application often involves spraying entire fields uniformly. This is inefficient and environmentally harmful. 

AI-powered precision sprayers use machine learning to distinguish between crops and weeds in real-time. As the tractor moves through the field, the system selectively sprays only the weeds, or applies fertilizer only to the plants that need it.

![Automated Drone Spraying](./assets/penyemprotan.jpg)
*Automated drones using AI to precisely apply treatments to specific areas of a field.*

This approach can reduce chemical usage by up to 80%, significantly lowering costs for farmers while drastically reducing the environmental impact of agricultural runoff.

## Predictive Yield and Weather Modeling

By combining historical yield data, real-time soil sensor data, satellite imagery, and complex weather forecasting models, AI can provide farmers with highly accurate yield predictions. This data helps in making informed decisions about:

- When to plant and harvest
- How much water to allocate
- Supply chain logistics and market pricing strategies

The future of farming is data-driven, ensuring global food security through intelligent, sustainable practices.
